# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary user: sole estimator and head of preconstruction. Future users: project managers, drafter, shop foreman — field-adjacent, context-switching constantly between screen and job site.

The estimator side is a precision instrument: dense data entry, keyboard-driven workflows, spreadsheet-speed tab-through. This is the engine. Every second it saves compounds across every bid.

The PM side is a dispatch board: when a PM opens it, they should be oriented in under three seconds. Next five action items, front and center. Not forms, not nav puzzles — just clarity about what needs attention right now.

## Product Purpose

A construction bid management and estimating suite. Covers the full preconstruction workflow: scoping areas and sections, pricing line items, managing alternates and exclusions, tracking opportunities through a pipeline, and handing off to PMs once a bid is won. Success looks like: bids built faster, nothing missed, and the PM team actually using the tool instead of sticky notes and phone calls.

## Brand Personality

Serious, precise, uncluttered. Not a startup product — a professional instrument. Feels like it was built by someone who has priced a lot of jobs and knows exactly what information matters at each step.

Three words: **confident, dense, clear**.

## Anti-references

Procore. The navigation, the information architecture, the everything. Users actively dislike it. This tool should be the opposite: fast to navigate, obvious where things live, never asking for more input than necessary.

No generic SaaS-blue gradient aesthetic. No white-and-teal construction management clichés.

## Design Principles

1. **Speed is a feature.** On the estimating side, every click removed and every tab-sequence optimized is real money saved. Keyboard-first, not click-first.
2. **Orient before asking.** On the PM side, the interface should tell users what matters before they have to ask. Status at a glance, action items surfaced, not buried.
3. **Density with clarity.** More information per pixel than a typical SaaS tool, but never noisy. Typographic hierarchy and color earn their weight by making the dense readable.
4. **No ceremony for routine tasks.** Routine data entry — adding a line item, logging a note, changing a status — should require zero confirmation dialogs, zero multi-step wizards.
5. **Different surfaces, different registers.** The workbook and PM dashboard are for different mental modes. Design choices should reinforce the switch, not blur it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Keyboard navigation is a first-class requirement on the estimating side (not just an a11y consideration — it's the primary interaction mode). Color contrast must hold across the status indicators (ok, warn, bad). No reduced-motion requirements stated yet; default to respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`.
